In this article, I will do a deep dive into Alphabet stock.

Alphabet Inc. the parent company of Google and Youtube, reported Q3 earnings on Oct 28th. What ensued was what CNBC dubbed Alphabet’s “worst day ever,” with the stock closing at about $283.82 on Oct 29, down 12.6%

Let’s dig into the Q3 results… what actually happened?

Positives:

On paper, the company missed badly on EPS, $2.12 vs. roughly $3.02 expected, but the EPS miss was due to an $8.7B paper loss on equity investments that cut EPS by about 55 cents. Revenue also came in practically in line at $127.6B (+25%). Thus, the headlines read much harsher than the actual news.

Cloud revenue was $30.7B (+103%), beating estimates, and operating income more than tripled to $11.1B (about a 36% margin). Growth at that scale with that margin is rare. They also reported a $561 billion backlog which gives multi-year clarity.

Core operations are also healthy. Operating income grew 30% to $40.6B at a 31.8% margin, and revenue growth accelerated to 25% from 16% a year ago.

Many believe that “AI kills search.” The numbers, however, say otherwise as search grew 12% with the highest query growth in five years. Let’s also not forget about YouTube and YouTube TV. YouTube is the top U.S. streaming platform, with 150M+ Music and Premium subs including trials.

Negatives:

The glaring headline was negative free cash flow, and that’s what shares got punished for. Q3 FCF was -$9.1B because capex of $51.4B (vs. $24B a year ago) exceeded operating cash flow of about $42B. This capex spend isn’t one-off either… it is only accelerating. Guidance is about $200B for 2026 and about $300B for 2027. A 50% increase on a record base means returns on invested capital have to hold up for years.

Outside of Cloud, growth is easing. Services grew 11% (vs. 15% in Q2), YouTube ads decelerated to 9% from 13%, and Network was roughly flat. Search & other missed consensus ($63.4B vs. about $65B).

Further, cloud margins will face pressure. Management flagged that third-party compute coming online in Q4 will weigh on Cloud margins, and depreciation is climbing as new infrastructure goes into service.

My takeaway:

The capex spend outlined above is concerning, but I’d like to mention a point. Capex is tied to backlog. The CFO described the sequence: a contract is signed and shows up in backlog, Google builds inventory and capacity ahead of revenue, and cash goes out before cash comes in. So negative FCF and rising backlog are connected, and the capex is partly pre-funded demand rather than speculative spend.

That gives a much more concrete avenue to return on spend. Because of this, I am leaning bullish on GOOG, especially at the current price ($283 as of Nov 2nd, 2026).

Three things would confirm the bull case:

  • FCF turning positive while Cloud growth stays above 60-70%.
  • Backlog converting to revenue on schedule, with limited concentration in a few AI labs.
  • Cloud margins holding after the Q4 compute ramp.

Three things would make me concerned:

  • Capex growth continuing to outpace Cloud revenue growth.
  • Search growth fading toward single digits while capex rises.
  • Depreciation compressing operating margin below the high 20s.
